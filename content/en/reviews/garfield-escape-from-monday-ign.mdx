## Gameplay: How the Monday Escape Loop Works

Based on how the premise is framed and how similar escape-style games are built, the loop likely follows a repeatable rhythm: enter a Monday-themed stage, identify the obstacles blocking progress, use Garfield's abilities to clear them, and reach an exit before the day "resets."

### The Core Loop, Step by Step

1. **Enter a stage** built around a Monday annoyance — alarms, traffic, chores, meetings.
2. **Read the environment** for the fastest route rather than the most obvious one.
3. **Use character abilities** to bypass or disable obstacles.
4. **Beat the clock** before the stage loops back to its start.
5. **Bank progress** and unlock the next layer of the escape.

## Tips and Strategies to Escape Monday Faster

These are the habits that separate players who breeze through Monday-themed stages from those who get stuck replaying the same section. They're general enough to apply whether the final build leans puzzle-heavy or action-heavy.

- **Learn the first ten seconds of every stage.** Opening segments are where most resets happen. Clear the start on autopilot and you keep your attention for the hard part.
- **Slow down before you speed up.** Timed hazards punish panic. Walk the route once, then optimize it.
- **Bank collectibles on a second pass.** Chasing optional pickups on your first run is how you run out of time.
- **Watch the clock at fixed intervals.** Checking a timer constantly breaks your focus on the route.
- **Practice the exit.** The last stretch of an escape is usually the tightest. Rehearse it deliberately.

| Priority | Strategy | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| High | Memorize stage openings | Prevents the most common early resets |
| High | Treat resets as reconnaissance | Turns failure into usable information |
| Medium | Delay collectible hunting | Protects your clear time on first attempts |
| Medium | Master one ability at a time | Builds muscle memory instead of button mashing |
| Low | Chase perfect runs early | Burns patience before you know the layout |

If you're the kind of player who bounces off a game after three failed attempts, give it five. Escape-style design usually clicks right after the frustration peak — and if it doesn't by then, the difficulty curve probably isn't for you.
