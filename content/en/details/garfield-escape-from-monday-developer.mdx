## How to Verify the Credit Yourself

If you have five minutes, you can settle the question without relying on anyone's comment section. Here is the sequence that works most reliably.

1. **Open the game and read the credits.** Look for a "made by," "created by," or "developed by" line. Screenshot it.
2. **Go back to the exact page you downloaded from.** The listing usually repeats the same name and may include a contact address.
3. **Cross-check the handle on two platforms.** Consistent handles across a portfolio and a store page are strong evidence.
4. **Look for devlogs or patch notes.** Update posts are almost always written by the developer in first person.
5. **Check the legal text.** A license notice or publisher logo points to an official release; an "unofficial fan project" disclaimer points the other way.

If steps 1 through 4 point to the same name and step 5 shows an unofficial disclaimer, you are almost certainly looking at a solo or small-team fan project. If a publisher logo appears instead, you are looking at something licensed, and the credit may belong to a studio rather than an individual.
